Expression-guided reflection

The camera requests native browser permission on arrival. A sustained expression reading automatically places a philosophical question beside the mirror and gently tints the glass. There is no launcher, camera button or modal. Questions consider flourishing, justice, freedom, knowledge, care, social norms and identity rather than asking for a diary entry. The writing container resembles ivory correspondence paper, with subtle procedural fibers, fine gilt borders and original botanical corner engraving. The first typed or pasted character locks the tab’s question, even if the response is later erased. Touching the mirror or pressing Command/Control+Enter sends the note into the glass; its paper shrinks and fades into the reflection. The note then disappears for the rest of that tab’s session. Keep a copy downloads the writing before it is sent.

Model and decision path

Human 3.3.6 runs MediaPipe BlazeFace detection and its bundled Oarriaga expression classifier in a dedicated worker using TensorFlow.js WASM 4.22.0. The library's model documentation identifies those models and their upstream sources. Only the detector and expression weights are shipped; identity, age, gender, body, hand and other analyses are disabled.

The classifier's seven expression categories route to broad writing themes. These are suggestions, not claims about the writer's internal state. Facial movements do not uniquely establish emotion; context and individual variation matter, as discussed in Barrett and colleagues' research review. No classification label or numerical confidence is displayed to the writer.

The collection contains 192 original prompts, with 24 in each of eight themes. Stable, explicitly assigned IDs identify questions independently of array order. Completed questions are excluded from selection. If a theme is exhausted, the selector tries unread open questions, then other unread themes with neutral glass. If the whole collection is exhausted, the mirror stays clear; the catalogue never cycles back through completed questions. The selector requires one detected face, detection confidence of at least 0.7, a face box at least 64 pixels wide and high, five independently inferred samples spanning at least 1.8 seconds, a mean winning score of at least 0.5, a margin of 0.18 over the next category, and agreement in at least 70% of samples. The rolling window retains at most ten samples from the last 4.5 seconds. Invalid quality or multiple faces clears the window. These thresholds are application heuristics, not calibrated estimates of emotion.

Model loading is limited to 20 seconds; observation is limited to 6.5 seconds after readiness. Frames are capped at 480 pixels on the longer side, with one inference in flight and at least 420 ms between observations. Frozen video timestamps do not accumulate evidence. Missing faces, ambiguity, multiple people and unavailable models use an open question initially, or preserve an already displayed question while returning the glass to neutral. Accessible status text explains the fallback without extra visible captions. A paused camera, stopped camera or hidden tab cancels the observation. After completion, another observation is scheduled after 14 seconds. Repeated readings of the same category retain the same question; a change has a minimum 12-second prompt hold. Focusing the editor temporarily suspends analysis. The first input ends automatic selection for the whole tab session, including camera restarts, tab visibility changes and page reloads. Erasing the response does not unlock selection.

Data and lifecycle

All runtime assets use the site's own origin. No camera image, expression score or writing is uploaded. The worker receives transient RGBA frames and returns only a small observation summary. It is terminated after a result, cancellation or failure, releasing its models and frame memory. Model persistence in IndexedDB is disabled. While the camera is active, bounded observations recur automatically, with no worker alive between observations. There is no expression analysis after writing has begun, after sending, or while the tab is hidden. The mirror camera continues during writing.

Escape stops the mirror camera and cancels observation. With a nonempty response, touching the mirror sends it; the mirror itself becomes an accessible keyboard action with Enter. Command/Control+Enter sends from the editor, while ordinary Enter remains a newline. Without a response, touching the mirror or pressing Enter resumes a stopped camera. The note never steals focus on arrival. Camera permission remains the browser’s native flow. Reduced-motion preferences replace the moving note with a short fade.

Session and completion history

A session is a browser tab, including reloads. sessionStorage keeps its locked prompt ID, hue association, draft and sent flag. Sending erases the draft from session storage, the editor and the downloadable in-memory blob. The durable history in localStorage contains only completed question IDs, never responses or camera images. Each ID has a separate key so two tabs cannot overwrite one another’s completions. Sending is idempotent and requires persistence to succeed before the paper leaves; a storage failure keeps the words visible and reports the problem.

A new tab can receive a new, unread question. An existing tab never receives another question after its first edit, including after sending. Completed questions remain excluded on this browser and site origin for as long as its storage is retained. There is no account or cross-device synchronization; clearing site data or using another browser resets that history. No identity or browser fingerprint is collected. A same-origin storage event replaces a retired question in an idle tab, but never interrupts an existing draft.

Color within the glass

The hue uses additional RGB absorption coefficients in inverse metres. Both traced trips through the glass include those coefficients, and the polished conductor finish is attenuated along the same glass path. This follows the existing layered-material light transport approach: the color does not add light, replace silver coverage, alter roughness or change the camera contribution. Front-surface glints remain neutral. At the 3 mm pane's normal-incidence round trip, the strongest coefficient removes about 11% of one channel. Longer angled paths naturally carry more color. A 1.1-second exponential response eases toward the selected hue; reduced-motion preferences apply it immediately.

These colors are artistic associations, not universal meanings or measurements of emotion. The palette never alters the frame, porcelain, cloth or lace. An uncertain result or camera stop restores the original silver.

Dressing-table objects

src/mirror/VanityObjects.ts adds original modeled objects under the mirror's existing HDR, key/fill lighting and shadows: a rolled brass hairbrush shell with a domed dark wooden insert, drilled tuft sockets, seven tapered and gently bent fibers per tuft, subtle natural color variation and a continuous rounded handle with fine botanical chasing; a scalloped porcelain dish with gold edging, a pearl bracelet and two gold rings, one with a rose-colored faceted stone; and a rounded, lidded porcelain trinket box. The ceramic has separate diffuse body and glossy clearcoat responses with small roughness variation. Painted floral marks are code-drawn texture detail under the glaze, mapped onto the curved surfaces.

The Met's 1848–49 hairbrush catalogue informs period handheld proportions and the distinction between backing and bristles. These objects are period-inspired designs, not reproductions of that ivory example. Gold and brass use the existing aged metal shading; ceramic geometry includes rounded walls, recessed wells and raised lips. The brush uses a continuous rounded casting from head through neck and handle, with fine palmette chasing seated on the curved throat. A slight roll exposes bristle depth. Grounding uses actual transformed mesh vertices, not the empty corners of rotated bounding boxes; a 0.1 mm cloth clearance keeps the cast shadow connected to the object on both desktop and mobile. The objects are grouped beside the mirror on wide screens and arranged beside the handle on narrow screens. The small box is omitted from the narrow arrangement to keep the oval large.

Model notices, licenses and checksums are in public/expression/. The repository README contains a one-sentence description and three screenshots of the desktop mirror, mobile writing, and completed reflection.

The 31 automated checks cover the existing mirror optics and media lifecycle, sustained expression evidence, ambiguity and quality resets, philosophical prompt routing, automatic cue/hue updates, permanent session locking after first input, reload restoration, late-result cancellation, sending, durable completion exclusion, concurrent tabs, exhausted collections and storage failure, actual model inference from simulated photographic video, no-face fallback, unavailable models, worker cancellation, writing/download behavior, keyboard flow and narrow-screen layout. GPU tests check both warm and cool absorption against the slab equations and verify that silver-loss regions retain their neutral front glint. The real-model test also checks same-origin requests and verifies that only the two intended models load. Its public-domain NASA test photograph is a development fixture, excluded from the production build.

Those checks establish integration, not population-level expression accuracy. The single photographic fixture does not cover different skin tones, ages, poses, occlusion or lighting. Cross-device latency and expression recognition need broader evaluation before claims of reliable emotion recognition. The current feature uses conservative observation thresholds, open-question fallbacks and no mental-health scoring or diagnosis.
